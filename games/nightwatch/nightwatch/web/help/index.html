    How NightWatch works, and what to do

    NightWatch watches for residents leaving their rooms or heading for an exit at night, and keeps raising the alarm until a person responds. Everything runs on the nurse-station laptop. Start with the quick card; the rest is for setting up and fixing things.

    On shift tonight

    The three levels, and what to do

    At night, a resident is up and it is unusual for them, at this time, or the hallway camera saw them step out of their room.
    Do: go and check when you can. Tap I'm going. The phone shows what has calmed this resident before.
    An exit door or window opened at night, the entrance camera saw someone, or a heads-up got no response.
    Do: go now. Tap I'm going to stop the climb.
    Still nobody answered, or a resident is confirmed outside.
    Do: respond immediately and follow your facility's missing-resident procedure. All phones alarm; the contact chain starts.

    Night is 21:00-05:00 on this system. During the day, doors opening are logged as visits and nothing alarms.

    Times on this page come from the running system.

    Escalation climbs only if nobody responds

    • →
    • no response for 45 s
    • →
    • →
    • not acknowledged in 25 s
    • →
    • A heads-up waits 45 seconds. If nobody taps I'm going and the resident is still up and unusual, it becomes urgent.
    • An urgent alert waits 25 seconds for an acknowledgement, then becomes an emergency.
    • At emergency, one contact step happens every 25 seconds until someone acknowledges: all staff phones alarm → on-call supervisor → emergency services per facility policy. In this build those calls are simulated: shown and logged, never dialled.
    • The ring on each alert counts down to the next step.
    • Once someone taps I'm going, the climb stops. If something new and urgent happens (for example the front door opens while you're on the way), it needs a fresh acknowledgement.
    • A heads-up clears itself if the resident settles back into bed on their own. A short ensuite trip normally stays quiet.

    "I'm going" vs "Found safe" vs "Need backup"

    I'm going

    You're on your way. Stops the escalation and tells everyone you're responding. The alert stays open.

    Found safe

    The resident is safe. Closes the alert and drafts the care record. NightWatch stays quiet about that resident for about 2 minutes while you settle them.

    Need backup

    You need another person. Tap twice (the first tap arms it). Every staff phone is alerted, and the alert goes to at least urgent.

    On the nurse-station laptop the same actions are buttons on each alert, or keys A (acknowledge) and F (found safe) for the top alert.

    Filing the care record

    When an alert closes, NightWatch drafts a care-record entry in the care team's own format: behaviour, location as a type (never a room number), the night band and the severity. You confirm the two things it can't know:

    1. How were they? (affect) CalmUnsettled-agitatedDistressed-tearfulDrowsyWithdrawnConfused-disorientedContent-engaged
    2. What did you do? (intervention) ReassuranceReorientationMusic/sensoryRepositioningPresence / 1-to-1Direct care providedClinical review
    3. Optional: a non-drug alternative you tried, and a short note.

    On the phone, the record sheet opens after Found safe. Tap Later if you're busy: it waits under Records to file. You can also file from the nurse station or the shift report. Your name is attached to the record.

    1Severity 1 · routineThe highest level reached was a heads-up.
    2Severity 2 · notableIt reached urgent (an exit, or no response to a heads-up).
    3Severity 3 · escalationIt reached emergency (nobody answered in time, or the resident got outside).

    Severity comes from the highest level the alert reached, not from how long it lasted. Staff can always add context in the note.

    Setting up

    Pair a staff phone with the PIN

    1. Connect the phone to the same Wi-Fi as the nurse-station laptop.
    2. Open http://<laptop address>:8000/staff/ in the phone's browser. The address is printed when NightWatch starts.
    3. Enter the 6-digit PIN shown on the nurse-station dashboard (also printed at startup), give the phone a name, and tap Pair.
    4. Tap Start shift to turn on sound and vibration, and enter your name. English and Spanish are available (EN / ES).

    You only pair once. The phone keeps a random token; the laptop stores only a hash of it. After 5 wrong PINs a phone must wait 60 seconds. The laptop itself never needs a PIN.

    For demos: --pin 123456 fixes the PIN; --unpair-all forgets every phone.

    Start the demo

    From the repository folder, one command starts the server on the local network with the best models available (NPU if built, otherwise CPU) and opens the nurse station, presenter and camera pages:

    powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File demo.ps1          # Windows (Snapdragon laptop)
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File demo.ps1 -Pin 246810
./demo.sh                                                  # macOS / Linux
PIN=246810 ./demo.sh

    Not sure what works on this machine? Run python nightwatch/check.py: it checks the models and prints the start command. To start by hand:

    python nightwatch/server.py --lan --model models/normal_pattern_xnnpack.pte --camera-model models/person_ssdlite_xnnpack.pte

    The demo uses short waits (--l1-timeout 20 --ack-timeout 15) so the ladder climbs quickly. Add --fresh to start a clean night instead of restoring tonight's saved alerts.

    Set up a hallway camera

    Open /camera/ on the laptop itself (browsers only allow webcams on localhost) and press Start camera. The setup guide walks you through it:

    1. Draw the doorway line. Press Draw doorway and click the two sides of the resident's doorway on the floor.
    2. Flip room side if needed: the side marked ROOM must be the resident's room. Only feet crossing from ROOM to HALLWAY count as leaving, so staff walking along the hallway don't trigger it. Crossing back in closes the heads-up.
    3. Test walk. Ask someone to walk out across the line, pause, and walk back in. The guide confirms both were seen. Do this before every demo.
    • Privacy view (blurred) is on by default. It only changes what this page shows; detection works the same.
    • Zoom on the doorway sends a crop around the door to the detector once a line is drawn, so people there are detected more reliably.
    • Night boost brightens dark hallway frames (capped, to avoid amplifying noise) before detection.
    • On / Off / Night only: each camera can be switched off or set to watch only at night (21:00-05:00), from the camera page or the nurse station. Off releases the webcam (its light goes off) and the server drops frames without looking at them. Every switch is logged with who did it.

    With no line drawn, a person seen in three frames in a row counts. The hallway camera outside room 12 means R-07 left their room (heads-up); the front-entrance camera means someone is at the exit (urgent).

    Settings page

    /settings/ (laptop only) edits the facility and night shift (name, night hours, waits), residents (including what has worked to calm them before and their spot on the floor plan), exits and cameras, with a live floor-plan preview.

    • Save and restart night applies the changes straight away and clears current alerts. Paired phones stay paired.
    • Settings are saved to data/facility.local.json on the laptop (never uploaded) and win over the demo file on the next start.
    • In a public demo, never type real resident names, health details or room numbers: demo residents are synthetic.

    Shift report

    /report/ is the morning handover: every alert tonight with response times, severity of closed alerts, alerts by resident, the care records (filed or still to file) and every camera privacy switch. Press Print for paper or PDF. Unfiled records can be filed right there.

    Performance page

    /perf/ shows how long each check and each camera frame takes on this laptop right now, and the latest benchmark runs (NPU vs CPU, energy, whether both give the same outputs). Benchmark the camera model runs a measurement while the demo keeps going. Unplug the laptop to measure energy.

    Troubleshooting

    The phone can't connect ("Server not reachable")

    • Same Wi-Fi: the phone must be on the same network as the laptop, not mobile data. Guest networks often block devices from reaching each other; use the staff network.
    • Started with --lan? Without it the server only answers the laptop itself. demo.ps1 and demo.sh add it for you.
    • Firewall: on Windows, allow Python on private networks when asked the first time (or in Windows Defender Firewall settings). Port 8000 must be reachable.
    • Right address: use the laptop's Wi-Fi address with :8000/staff/, printed at startup. localhost on a phone means the phone itself.

    "Pair this phone" keeps appearing

    The laptop doesn't recognise this device. Enter the PIN from the nurse-station dashboard. This happens on a new phone, after clearing the browser's site data or using a private tab, or after someone ran --unpair-all. If it says to wait, 5 wrong PINs were entered: try again in 60 seconds.

    Camera permission blocked, or camera in use

    • Permission blocked: click the camera or lock icon left of the address bar, set Camera to Allow, press Try again. On Windows also check Settings → Privacy & security → Camera.
    • In use by another app: Windows lets one app use the webcam at a time. Close Teams, Zoom, the Camera app or another tab using it, then Try again.
    • Needs localhost: open the camera page on the laptop at http://localhost:8000/camera/, not from another device.
    • No camera found: plug in the USB webcam or enable the built-in one.

    Model not loaded (rule fallback)

    If the pattern model fails to load, the server says so at startup and keeps watching with fixed night rules (left the room, exit opened, outside). Doors and the escalation ladder still work; only the "unusual for right now" judgement is missing. The dashboard always shows which model and device are live.

    If the camera page says the detector is not loaded, start the server with --camera-model models/person_ssdlite_xnnpack.pte (the demo scripts do). Run python nightwatch/check.py to see what's missing.

    The server restarted

    Tonight's alerts (with history, acknowledgements and care records), events, camera switches, doorway lines and the clock are saved on the laptop about a second after every change, and restored when the server starts again on the same night. Paired phones stay paired; open pages reconnect by themselves. If the PIN was random it will be new, but already-paired phones don't need it.

    The camera page must be started again on the laptop after a restart. Use --fresh only if you want an empty night.

    Wi-Fi or the internet is off

    NightWatch needs no internet. The models, pages and alerts all run on the laptop, so the nurse station keeps working with Wi-Fi off and shows Offline · still protecting. Phones need the local Wi-Fi to reach the laptop: if the facility Wi-Fi itself is down, watch the nurse-station screen and follow your facility's backup procedure.

    Privacy and families

    What does the camera see, and what does it keep?

    A camera frame goes from the browser to the laptop's memory, a person detector finds where people are, and the frame is dropped straight away. Nothing is recorded: no video, no photos, nothing sent anywhere.

    What's kept is only where a person is and whether their feet crossed the doorway line, for the last few seconds. The nurse station shows a dot on a floor plan, never video. The camera page on the laptop shows a live view for setup, blurred by default. NightWatch does not recognise faces or identify people; it only knows which camera saw a person.

    Who can see alerts?

    The nurse-station laptop and staff phones that were paired with the PIN, on the facility's own Wi-Fi. Any other device is refused. Alerts show a resident's code and name as set up by the facility, what happened, and what has helped before.

    Where does the data live?

    • On one laptop in the building. Nothing goes to the cloud; no internet is needed.
    • Tonight's alerts, events and care-record drafts are kept on the laptop so a restart doesn't lose the night, and replaced as the next night begins (or when a demo scenario or new settings restart the night).
    • Facility settings and the list of paired phones (hashes only) are files on the laptop.
    • Care records are drafts for staff to confirm; the facility decides what goes into its official records.

    How do I turn cameras off, or night only?

    Each camera has an On / Off switch and Always / Night only on the camera page and the nurse station. Off means off: the webcam is released (its light goes off) and nothing is analysed. Night only watches 21:00-05:00. Every change is logged with who made it and appears in the shift report. Families can ask the facility for a camera near their relative to be night-only or off.

    What is simulated in this demo?

    • Residents are synthetic. Every name and number is invented.
    • The contact chain is simulated: it never calls or texts anyone. Steps are shown and logged as "(simulated)".
    • The presenter's camera walk-out scenario uses simulated camera data and is labelled SIMULATED on screen.
    • Sensors (doors, bed, heart rate) come from the simulator unless real hardware is connected.
    • The "is this unusual for right now?" model learned normal from simulated days; in a facility it would learn each resident's own pattern.
    Not a medical device. NightWatch supports night staff; it does not replace them, their judgement or the facility's procedures.

    A plain-language notice for families is in the repository at docs/PRIVACY_NOTICE.md (a template to adapt with the facility's privacy officer).
